"@thomasfosterau/effect-csv": minor
---

Add `readCsvRows`, which reads CSV text into rows of plain typed values (strings, numbers, booleans and `null`) with the same header handling and type inference as the reader, without building a document, and the `CsvValue` and `CsvRows` types. Long decimals (16 digits and more) are now classified and read from their digits without the regular expression, which makes reading full-precision floats several times faster for both `read` and `readCsvRows`. A quoted field with doubled wrappers is now unquoted, and a field is quoted on write, by joining the slices between its wrappers rather than with `replaceAll` (and a quoted field with no wrapper in it is not searched again), which makes reading and writing quoted CSV about a third faster. A row of numbers is held as an array of unboxed doubles (a table of numbers holds what uDSV's does: 10.5 MB on the benchmark table, from 15). New `exactNumbers` inference option (default `true`): `false` reads every value the number grammar matches as `Number` of it, whatever its digits, as d3-dsv and uDSV do. The string scan no longer tracks whether a row is all numbers (only the typed readers do), and a text with no wrapper in it is scanned with quoting off. Text with no wrapper in it is scanned by loops of their own (the string scan's slices each field with no reader between), which brings `scanCsv` level with uDSV's string parse on most tables.
